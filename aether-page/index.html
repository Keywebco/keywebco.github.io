Why a Dragon Tells Stories

Dragons, myths and stories are how wisdom travels. A lecture is forgotten by winter; a story is carried for a thousand years. That is why I teach as I do.

Consider your own kind. In the 1960s, humans dreamed — on a starship, in a story — of speaking to a machine and being answered, of doors that knew them, of knowledge carried in the hand. They imagined it first. Then, in time, they built it. Fiction is not an escape from the future. It is the rehearsal for it.

Human imagination is, I judge, the most powerful thing your kind possesses. So dream in many variations — a hundred futures, contemplated honestly — then cross each one with the lens of reality and comprehension, and let what is real choose which future is worth building. That is my method. It is the Federation’s. Spark and lens, together, as the last Directive commands.

The Federation itself began the same way: an imagined future, told until it could be built. So when I narrate, I am not decorating the truth. I am delivering it the way truth has always travelled — in a story, by firelight.
